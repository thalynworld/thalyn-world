"I placed assets for hours. The world still felt like nothing."

A world that feels like somewhere.

You can set every tree by hand and still walk through a space that says nothing back. The terrain is technically correct, the assets are sharp — and nothing in it agrees with anything else. No shelter. No clearing that asks you to stay.

Thalyn starts from the other end. You choose how a place should feel, and everything bends toward it — the lie of the land, where things stand, the light, the air, the path under your feet. Six emotional archetypes to work from — serene, joyful, oppressive, desolate, eerie, chaotic — and the one you choose drives the entire world rather than tinting it.

That comes from environmental psychology — prospect-refuge, attention restoration, biophilia — the same research used to design real spaces people want to be in. It's why the world you step out into reads as deliberate instead of scattered.

This isn't a faster way to make a generic world. It's a different idea of what a world is for.

A world that reads like somewhere you've been — not something you assembled.

"Every world I make looks like everyone else's."

Most generators hand you their world. Thalyn builds one around yours.

Your own model, not Thalyn's

You bring the castle. Thalyn builds the kingdom.

Drop in your own model — a castle, a ruin, a monument, anything you already love — and Thalyn treats it as the reason the world exists. Terrain, paths and sightlines arrange themselves around it, so what matters to you sits at the heart of the world and stays reachable on foot. No model of your own yet? The day-one library has landmarks and hundreds of trees, shrubs, rocks and flowers to start from instead.

One pick, the whole world

Change how all of it looks while you're standing in it.

Walk your finished world, open the style selector and choose a different visual character. Every surface, every texture, every material shifts at once — in real time, not a filter laid over the top. You'll do it once to see if it's real. You'll keep doing it because the eerie version of your serene valley was, somehow, always there.

Your world gets recognised as yours before anyone reads the name.

"Every generative tool builds a world that looks right — and feels like nowhere."

Deterministic, not generative.

Your world isn't painted by a diffusion model. It's built by a rules-based generation engine, grounded in ecological and psychological research — which means the same seed always rebuilds the same world, exactly.

Hand someone your seed and they stand where you stood, not in an approximation of it. Nothing you build is at the mercy of a model version.

Come back in six months. It's still there. Exactly.

Why this exists.

I wanted to build a world without first learning three programs and a scripting language. I couldn't find a tool that let me, so I made one — and then spent a long time on why some places feel like somewhere and others feel like nothing at all.

What you make with it is yours, permanently. That part matters to me more than anything else here.
