The part that doesn't have to be hard

Less time assembling landscapes. More time making your game.

Creating an atmosphere that holds together can take time away from the experience you want to make. Thalyn gives small teams and independent developers a way to compose and explore whole scenes without starting every environment from an empty viewport.

Terrain takes time. Placing assets takes longer. Getting the two to feel like one coherent place — not a pile of placed things — takes experience and hours you would rather spend on the game itself.

Begin with Thalyn's included assets and choose a mood. Build and edit your scene, explore it in first person, and export the result to glTF. Importing your own FBX models is planned for the main release, currently targeted for March–April 2027, rather than the November 2026 demo.

For concept work and prototyping, use world seeds to revisit ideas and test different moods. For production, inspect the exported geometry, materials and scale in your own pipeline before deciding how to use them.

The week you were going to lose to the landscape goes into the build your players remember.

What you walk away with

Included Assets, Your Arrangement

Start with Thalyn's included structures, foliage and scenery and arrange a world around your idea. Bringing your own FBX models is planned for the full release.

You Own the Output

Keep, modify and commercially distribute your finished exported worlds under Thalyn's licence. The included raw asset library remains licensed and cannot be extracted and resold separately as an asset pack.

Iterate on Whole Worlds

Try a different version of a world in a single session, without rebuilding from scratch. Every world you make is saved, so the one you shelved in March is still waiting when the design changes.

Coherent, Not Scattered

Landscape, atmosphere and placement respond to the world mood and chosen landmarks. You can then build, edit and explore what has been made.

Built for Your Team

Hand a world to a teammate and they open exactly what you made — same terrain, same placement, down to the detail. Reviews stop being screenshots of near-enough copies.

Exports for your workflow

A Roblox-specific export route is under compatibility testing. We won't promise perfect transfer of lighting, weather, collision or performance until the resulting Roblox scenes have been verified.

All of it leaves with you, into your build and in front of your players.

Deterministic, not generative

Revisit the generated world you chose.

Thalyn's seeded, rules-based approach is designed to reproduce generated landscapes from the same seed and settings. It draws on environmental psychology when arranging spaces and moods. Keep the seed and settings with your project to revisit the starting world.

That matters at the moments a pipeline usually hurts. A bug report arrives with a seed and you are standing in the tester's world, not an approximation of it. A build from March opens unchanged in September. Nothing you build is at the mercy of a model version.

It matters for what you ship, too. A splat is a photograph of a world. You can't hand a photograph to your players — they need real geometry, real materials, and a scene your engine can open. That is what comes out of Thalyn.

Six months from now, your whole team opens the same world you did. Exactly.

Out of Thalyn, into your build

Take it out, and let someone else walk it.

Export completed worlds as glTF files and take them into compatible tools such as Blender, Unity and Three.js. A browser-based World Showcase is being prepared to demonstrate real exports on thalyn.world. Roblox-specific export is being tested separately; results may need adaptation inside Roblox Studio.

Exporting is free and unlimited. It's a pipeline step, not a generation, so the same world can go out again after every change — and again the day you finally ship it.

A world only you have walked is just a place. Export is how it becomes somewhere.
