What that means

Imagine a place. Make it yours. Move in.

Begin with a feeling — peaceful, joyful, eerie or something of your own.

Make it yours — use the included library, shape paths and build places you want to be, while the world comes together around your choices.

The problem it solves

"I can picture exactly how it should feel. I just can't build it."

Making a world has always meant making a thousand decisions.

The usual way, you shape the terrain, script the systems, hunt down the assets, light the scene, then lose days fitting the pieces together. And when you finally stand in it, it still reads as a collection of objects rather than a place.

Here you make one choice — how it should feel — and the rest resolves underneath you into a single, coherent, walkable world. Not a folder of parts to assemble.

Start with the included buildings and scenery, then add your own style. Personal FBX importing is planned for the full release.

A world, grown from one feeling. Footage coming soon.

How it works

Three steps from an idea to a place of your own.

There's no syllabus and no timeline of tasks to work through. You say it, you place what matters to you, and you walk in.

1

Say how it should feel

Out loud, typed, or picked from a menu — in the words you'd use to describe the place to a friend. That's the whole brief.

2

Place what you love

Start with a cottage, tower, landmark or landscape from the included asset collection, then build the details that make it yours. Personal FBX importing is planned for the full release after February 2027 Next Fest.

3

Walk in.

You're standing in it, in first person. Change its entire look in a single pick. When it feels right, take it with you — into Roblox, or into whatever you're building next.

When you've made somewhere worth staying, the next thing you'll want to do is show someone.

What makes it different

A world that feels like somewhere.

Plenty of tools can fill a scene. Here you end up somewhere — a place with a mood you can stand inside, where the land, the light and everything in it belong to the same feeling.

A colour grade changes the colour. A mood changes everything — the colour, the light, the weather, the water, what grows and what lives there. Six moods. Same seed, six different places.

That comes from real study of how spaces shape the way people feel, turned into worlds you can walk. It's why your world reads as deliberate rather than scattered — and why nobody you show it to has to be told what it was meant to be.

Grounded, not guessed

The mood is the point — and it holds up close.

Every world is shaped around the feeling you chose, from the widest horizon down to the path under your feet. If you want the reasoning behind why a space lands the way it does, it is all written down. Read the research →

A world that reads like somewhere you've been — not something you assembled.
